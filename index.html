Phone Wait Times Are Actively Driving Members to Competitors
At least seven detractor comments in January alone cite multi-hour hold times, inability to reach anyone across multiple days, confusing IVR routing, and inability to connect directly to a local branch. Several members state they would not recommend the credit union specifically because of phone access. For a CU whose brand promise is personal service, this is a direct contradiction.

Phone is the highest-volume service channel for members 40+, which is Copper State's current membership core. Reducing average wait time below 3 minutes is a measurable, achievable retention lever.
"Telephone lines always busy, sat on hold for several hours and never got through to anyone. Tried for several days." · "Actually have somebody that picks up the phone when someone calls."
Phone Avg Rating: 4.36/5 · 301 responses~7 explicit detractor mentions this month
Not Started Digital
Digital Experience Is a Growth Ceiling, Not Just a Low Score
Digital Experience scored 51.89% — Off Track and the single lowest-rated channel in the January report. Member comments describe the website as "awful," "confusing," "forced upon us," and not user-friendly. The mobile app is missing card lock/unlock, has slow check deposit, and requires repeated re-authentication for Zelle. These are standard features at competing institutions.

For aggressive five-year growth, digital is the primary acquisition channel for members under 40. A substandard digital product makes it nearly impossible to grow in the millennial and Gen Z segments, which represent the largest generational wealth transfer in history.
"Your website is awful. I have mentioned it so many times. It's not that hard to have a user friendly easy website — it's 2026." · "Allow members to shut off their card through the app."
Digital CSAT: 51.89% · 106 responsesOnly channel rated Off Track (raw score: 1)
Not Started Chat
Autopilot Chat Is Eroding Trust in the Digital Channel
Chat scored 2.50/5 in January (Mixed), but the underlying data reveals that Autopilot (AI bot) scores 1.05–2.00/5 while human agents score 3.0–4.75/5. The combined score masks a severe automation failure. Members are explicitly telling the survey they received "no help" and are redirecting to branches — the highest-cost service channel.

At scale, a poorly performing AI chatbot does not save cost; it transfers cost to branches while actively damaging satisfaction. A human-first chat routing model with AI assist — rather than AI-first deflection — would immediately improve scores and protect branch capacity as the member base grows.
"No help." · "Did not help — will go to my branch." · "Learn AI."

Fraud Response Failures Create the Highest-Risk Churn Moments
Three distinct fraud-related member complaints appear in January: one member lost $6,500 when the fraud department refused to speak with her by phone; another reports card replacement after fraud detection takes "way, way too long"; a third was told nothing could be done about CSCU phone numbers being spoofed by scammers. In banking, a poor fraud response is one of the strongest predictors of immediate account closure and negative word-of-mouth.

For a credit union competing on trust versus megabanks, a faster and more empathetic fraud response is a direct competitive advantage. Same-day card replacement and accessible fraud intake are table-stakes for growth-oriented institutions.
"I was scammed out of $6,500 because your fraud dept refused to speak to me on the phone." · "It takes way, way too long to get a replacement card."

Physical Infrastructure Reliability Undermines the In-Branch Experience
Specific infrastructure failures appear repeatedly: a drive-thru machine that is "always down," a drive-thru call button inaccessible from standard vehicles at the Dysart branch, an ATM placement that forces large vehicles to exit their car, and debit cards that fail at point-of-sale. Each failure is minor in isolation but cumulative in perception. Members who cite these issues are already advocates (scores of 7–8) who are being held below promoter status by preventable operational gaps.

A systematic audit of physical touch-point reliability — ATMs, drive-thrus, card authorization rates — with branch-level SLAs would directly convert passives to promoters. These fixes tend to have high NPS ROI relative to cost.
"Your drive-thru machine is always down." · "The drive-up call button is in a position that neither my car nor truck can access without getting out." · "I constantly have to use another card because yours does not work."

Geographic Footprint Is Capping Referral Growth
Multiple promoters and passives explicitly state they would have scored higher — or do not recommend — because Copper State has no presence in the East Valley (Scottsdale, Tempe, Mesa, Fountain Hills, Chandler). One member drives 25 minutes to the Surprise branch for cash withdrawals and uses Desert Financial on Saturdays instead. Another member noted family can't join because they are not Arizona-eligible.

The East Valley represents approximately 40% of the Phoenix metro population and is the fastest-growing submarket. Members who are already loyal are blocked from referring their networks. Each new branch in underserved areas unlocks a referral multiplier from the existing member base.
"It would be a 10 if you had branches in the east valley." · "Have an office on the east side of the valley for convenience to people in Scottsdale, Fountain Hills, Tempe, Mesa."

Weekend and Extended Hour Gaps Are Preventing Primary Account Capture
At least six detractor and passive comments cite weekend closures or early weekday closures as the explicit reason they cannot use Copper State as their primary financial institution. One member deposits tip income at Desert Financial on Saturdays because she cannot get to a branch on weekdays. Another member gave a 4 rating specifically because Saturday access is unavailable.

For members in service, retail, or hourly-wage industries — a substantial portion of Arizona's working population — M-F banking hours mean CSCU is structurally ineligible to be their primary bank. Saturday hours at select high-traffic locations would directly increase primary account penetration without requiring new branches.

Technology Transition Friction Is Creating Avoidable Attrition
Multiple comments reference confusion and frustration stemming from the rollout of the new online banking interface "about a year ago." Started All Channels
Rate Competitiveness Is a Threshold Attribute — Silent Attrition Risk
Several passives and detractors dropped their NPS score specifically because of rate changes: one stopped using his CSCU credit card after an unexplained 4%+ rate increase and switched to OneAZ; another now gets lower auto rates and higher savings rates from a competing credit union; a third moved his mortgage elsewhere. Importantly, some promoters chose CSCU because rates were the best — meaning rate positioning drives both acquisition and retention.

The pattern of unexplained rate increases is more damaging than the increases themselves. Members tolerate rate changes when communicated proactively with context. A rate change communication strategy — brief, transparent, ahead of implementation — is a low-cost trust investment with measurable NPS impact.
"My credit card rate was raised over 4% with no explanation. I stopped using it." · "I used to get the lowest rates through you. Lately I've been getting lower auto rates through another local credit union."
Cross-channel product issueProactive communication reduces detractor conversion

Started Branch
Small Fee and Policy Friction Points Are Converting Promoters to Passives
Inactivity fees, coin counter charges, the elimination of free cashier's checks, and restrictions on cash withdrawals without advance notice appear as specific irritants in comments from members who otherwise have positive relationships. One member with a $180k balance was refused a $3–5k cash withdrawal without pre-scheduling — a policy that led to a near-defection. Another is reconsidering membership over a $10 inactivity fee.

These are low-revenue, high-friction policies. Members are not price-sensitive about relationship banking — but they are highly sensitive to policies that signal distrust or bureaucratic indifference. Revisiting fees and cash access policies with a "member-first" lens would produce measurable NPS gains at minimal revenue cost.
"Branch was not allowing me to take out $3k–$5k without scheduling the withdrawal in advance with a $180k balance." · "I have a $10 inactive fee charged — this causes me to think about moving."

Multi-Generational Loyalty Is an Underutilized Growth Engine
Dozens of promoter comments reference membership tenure of 20, 30, 40, or 50+ years — dating back to Deer Valley FCU, Grand Canyon CU, SAFCO, and DES Credit Union. Members explicitly name their children and grandchildren as fellow members. This depth of generational loyalty is exceptional in financial services and represents a built-in referral network that is currently informal and unsystematized.

A structured member-get-member program with incentives tied to household growth would convert passive loyalty into active acquisition. Long-tenure members are also the most credible advocates during new product launches or rate campaigns.

Embedding co-op branch/ATM locator tools in the app and website, and scripting the benefit into frontline conversations, would reduce location-based detraction without capital expenditure.

Loan Experience Is a Proven Acquisition and Retention Engine
Consumer Loan CSAT hit 100% in January (15 responses) and Mortgage reached 80% YTD. Member comments across NPS data frequently cite auto loans, HELOCs, and mortgages as the reason they originally joined or why they stayed — sometimes 20–30 years later. Multiple members reference 3, 4, or 5 auto loans over their membership. The loan experience is not just a product line; it is the primary acquisition event for a large share of the membership.

A loan-to-full-relationship conversion strategy — systematically offering checking, savings, and digital enrollment at loan origination — would significantly increase wallet share per acquired member. The trust is already established at the loan moment; conversion is a process design problem, not a relationship problem.

Ask AI  ·  January 2026
Top 10 AI Insights
1
Autopilot Chat is the most urgent single fix in the entire MEME scorecard
Chat Jan 2025 – Jan 2026
Autopilot scored 1.05–2.00/5 across every month of 2025, while human agents scored 3.0–4.75/5 in the same channel. The combined Jan 2026 score of 2.50 masks this split entirely. Autopilot is actively pulling down the overall MX score — and members are telling us why: "no help," "learn AI," "did not help will go to my branch."
2
Digital platform migration created a satisfaction crater that is still recovering 12 months later
Digital Feb 2025 – Jan 2026
Digital dropped from ~69% (Dec 2024) to 20–29% (Feb–Mar 2025) at platform launch and is at 51.89% in January 2026 — still 17 points below pre-migration baseline. At the current recovery rate of ~3 points per month, full recovery will take another 6–12 months. Login failures and the mobile app are the top complaint themes.
3
Named employees are the #1 predictor of a positive comment across every channel
Branch Phone New Loan New Member Jan 2026
In January 2026, 14 of 35 member voice comments specifically name an employee — Harmony, Thomas, Carolyn, Skylar, Eileen, Jordon, Yesi, Johanna, Liliana, Anthony, Carol, Stephanie, Sierra, and Gabriela. Every named mention is positive. Channels where staff are named score significantly higher than channels where they aren't.
4
Branch has held Exceptional tier for 24+ consecutive months — it is the operational model
Branch Jan 2024 – Jan 2026
Branch reached 98.95% in January 2026 and has been at or above 95% in every month with recorded data since early 2024. Member comments consistently cite friendly staff, consistency across visits, and employees going above expectations. No other channel has maintained Exceptional tier for this duration.
5
Speed and ease of process are what loan members care about most
New Loan Mortgage Jan 2026
Words like "quick," "easy," "convenient," and "smooth" appear in 8 of 14 loan and mortgage comments this month. Members who mention processing speed are unanimous in their satisfaction. This is consistently the top theme in loan feedback — operational efficiency is the most actionable lever for loan satisfaction scores.
6
NPS hit an all-time high of 74 — and the promoter majority is growing
NPS Q3 2022 – Jan 2026
NPS has trended 67 → 62 → 72 → 72 → 74 since Q3 2022. Promoters now represent 81% of respondents. The Q2 2024 dip to 62 appears to have been a temporary outlier, not a trend. Crossing 80 (Exceptional tier) is achievable in 2026 if Digital and Chat recover, since dissatisfied members in those channels directly suppress NPS.
7
The 26.2% overall response rate is a statistical illusion — the real blended rate is ~12%
Response Rate Jan 2026
January's 26.2% headline is boosted by Mortgage's 83.3% response rate on just 6 delivered surveys. Remove Mortgage and the true blended rate across the remaining five channels is approximately 11–12%, consistent with prior months. Tracking response rate as a single aggregate number without channel context is misleading.
8
New Member scores are highly volatile at current sample sizes — monthly results are unreliable
New Member Jan 2025 – Jan 2026
Monthly New Member scores range from 50% (Feb 2025, 2 happy out of 4) to 100% (Sep, Nov 2025). With 5–15 responses per month, a single unhappy member shifts the score by 7–20 points. The 12-month trailing average of 92.31% is statistically meaningful; the monthly score is not. Confidence interval is ±15 points at current volume.
9
Phone experience had an unexplained Q2 2025 dip that warrants a root cause review
Phone Apr – May 2025
Phone dropped from 4.53/5 in January 2025 to 3.63–3.66/5 in April–May 2025 — a 0.9-point decline — then recovered to 4.36 by January 2026. The cause is unknown. Possible factors include seasonal call volume spikes, staffing changes, or a specific complaint driver. If it recurs in Q2 2026, it becomes a pattern worth escalating.
10
The Digital survey is capturing in-branch experiences — creating a contaminated data signal
Digital Branch Jan 2026
Two of four positive Digital comments in January describe in-person branch visits at the Surprise location and an in-branch account setup — interactions that have nothing to do with the website or app. If this is a recurring pattern in the pop-up survey, Digital's satisfaction score is artificially inflated by branch goodwill, masking the true digital experience quality.
